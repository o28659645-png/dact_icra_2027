DACT: Dynamics-Aware Learning of Coupled Torque Policies for Disturbance-Robust Legged Locomotion

Paper Supplementary Material
IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA) 2027

(Under Review)

Abstract

Robust quadruped deployment requires policies that reject disturbances from payloads, terrain variation, and torso-level external forces and moments. However, standard position-control policies compensate only indirectly through fixed PD feedback, while direct-torque policies often lose high-bandwidth stabilization, and auxiliary compensation methods add additional training/compute without resolving the underlying action space-dynamics representation mismatch. We introduce DACT, a dynamics-aware framework that embeds disturbance compensation directly into the locomotion policy via coupled feedforward-torque and feedback-stabilized position actions. DACT learns a privileged-dynamics-aware latent context through next-step privileged observation reconstruction and uses physics-informed neural network (PINN) regularization to bias actions toward physically consistent whole-body stabilization. We evaluate DACT across payload variation, CoM shifts, complex terrain, and 6-DOF torso wrenches, with real-world tests including dynamic liquid payload transport across unstructured outdoor terrain. Results show that DACT improves disturbance rejection and locomotion stability over position-control, direct-torque, and adaptive torque-compensation baselines, enabling robust real-world locomotion under diverse external disturbances.

Motivation

Quadruped robots have achieved substantial advances in locomotion capabilities, enabling agile mobility through unstructured environments that remain difficult or inaccessible for wheeled platforms. Reinforcement learning (RL) has further expanded these capabilities by training robust gait policies in large-scale simulation, reducing reliance on hand-designed models while supporting dynamic behaviors across diverse terrains and commands. However, real-world deployments rarely occur under nominal conditions: robots may carry supplies over uneven terrain, recover from contact with obstacles or humans, or maintain balance as onboard loads shift during motion. These disturbances perturb body motion, reshape contact forces, and disrupt the coupling between foot placement, force generation, and torso stabilization. Robust deployment therefore requires policies that reject disturbances online while preserving stable and reliable locomotion across diverse operating conditions.
